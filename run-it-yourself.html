Run it yourself

How to set up the Keepy Up software on your own laptop: the ceiling camera, the taped arena, the robot markers, the balloon and the robots. One arena and one tracker run both games: keepy uppy and floor soccer. The build guide covers what to buy.

1. What you need

  • A Mac laptop with Python 3 (the code was built with Python 3.9). Other computers should work, but the steps here are for a Mac.
  • A USB webcam on the ceiling. Ours is the EMEET SmartCam C960 (1080p).
  • Cozmo, and a MiP with Bluetooth.
  • A yellow balloon. Ours is about 13 in (33 cm) wide.
  • Floor tape in three colours: purple, pink and blue.
  • Small pieces of coloured tape for the robots: green, pink and blue.
  • A tape measure.

The build guide has the full shopping list, with why each item is needed.

2. Install the software

Unzip the download, open Terminal, and run these one at a time. They assume the folder is in Downloads.

cd ~/Downloads/keepy-up-code
python3 -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r tracker/requirements.txt
pip install vosk sounddevice pillow certifi
python3 -m pycozmo.resources download

The last line downloads Cozmo's animation files, once. The voice demo downloads a small English voice model (about 40 MB) the first time it runs, and after that works offline.

Every time you open a new Terminal window, start with:

cd ~/Downloads/keepy-up-code
source .venv/bin/activate

When macOS asks to let Terminal use the camera, microphone or Bluetooth, choose Allow.

3. Put up the camera

Fix the webcam to the ceiling, pointing straight down. In our room:

  • the ceiling is 6'-10¼" (2.09 m) high
  • the camera lens is 6'-6" (1.98 m) above the floor
  • the lens sees about 82° across, which covers the whole 10 × 5⅓ ft arena

Measure from the floor to the lens with a tape measure and write it down: you need it in steps 4 and 5. The top of the camera picture is called the back of the arena, and the bottom the front.

Which camera number? The commands below use --source 0, which is our EMEET. If a command shows the wrong camera (for example, the laptop's own), try --source 1.

Can't use the ceiling? A corner clamp also works

Clamp the webcam high in a corner and tilt it down, as in Part 1 of the build guide. The tracker works out the camera's height and angle itself, so leave out --camera-height in step 5. The steps on this page were tested with the ceiling camera.

4. Tape the arena

Both games share one arena. Ours is 10'-0" wide by 5'-4" deep (3.05 × 1.63 m), measured along the middle of the purple tape.

TapeWhat it marksGame
PurpleThe border, and a cross at each corner. Cross 1 is back-left, 2 back-right, 3 front-right and 4 front-left.Both
PinkThe centre line. In keepy uppy it splits Cozmo's half (left) from MiP's (right); in soccer it's the line nobody crosses.Both
PinkA goal 72 cm wide in the middle of each short end: a post at each side and the goal line between them.Floor soccer
PinkTwo 30 cm squares just inside the robots' goal, where the robots wait: Cozmo by the top (back) post, MiP by the bottom (front) post.Floor soccer

Let the camera show you where to tape

tape_guide.py draws the tape marks on the live camera picture, so you can tape exactly where they appear. Run it from the tracker folder, with your own lens height:

cd tracker
python tape_guide.py --source 0 --camera-height 1.98                 # keepy uppy marks
python tape_guide.py --source 0 --camera-height 1.98 --game soccer   # floor soccer marks

While it's running, + and - make the arena wider or narrower, ] and [ make it deeper or shallower, G switches games, and Q quits. When you quit, it prints where each mark goes, measured from cross 1. The sizes on screen are approximate, so measure the real distances once the tape is down.

5. Teach the tracker the floor

This tells the tracker where the floor is, in metres. Redo it whenever the camera moves. From the tracker folder, give your measured arena size and lens height:

python floor_setup.py --source 0 --width 3.05 --depth 1.63 --camera-height 1.98

A window shows the camera picture. Click the 4 purple crosses in order: 1 back-left, 2 back-right, 3 front-right, 4 front-left. Press U to undo a click, S to save, or Q to quit.

After saving, check two things:

  • It prints how high it thinks the camera is. That should match your tape measure.
  • Open floor_check.jpg in the tracker folder. Its 50 cm grid should sit on your tape, and a thick line marks the middle. Ours fits the tape within about 5 cm at the worst corner, well inside the robots' 20 cm safety margin from the border.

If either looks wrong, check the click order and try again.

Optional: straighten the lens first (more accurate)

Do this once per camera, before the floor step. Print a checkerboard with 9 × 6 inner corners and 25 mm squares, then run:

python floor_setup.py --source 0 --lens

Hold the checkerboard in front of the camera. Press Space each time the corners are drawn in colour, about 15 times, at different angles and distances, and include the edges of the picture. Then press C to calculate.

6. Mark the robots

From the ceiling, the camera tells the robots apart by coloured tape on top:

  • a green patch (3–4 cm) on top of each robot
  • a smaller front piece, about 3 cm, on the side the robot faces: pink for Cozmo, blue for MiP

The front piece tells the tracker which robot it is and which way it's facing. Don't use yellow or orange on the robots, because they look too much like the balloon. Use tape that peels off cleanly and keep it off the wheels, lift and sensors.

With this tape, the tracker found Cozmo in 98% of camera frames and MiP in 100%. Green things outside the arena are ignored.

When the balloon is right over a robot, the camera can't see that robot. The tracker keeps its last position for up to 2 seconds, and the robot holds still until it's seen again. This is on purpose, for safety.

Why not printed AprilTags?

We first tried printed AprilTags (4 cm on Cozmo, 5 cm on MiP). From a 6½ ft ceiling they were too small: the camera read them in only 1–14% of frames. The tracker still reads a tag if it can see one, which may help with a camera closer to the floor, or with bigger tags.

↓ Download the robot tags (PDF, 1 page)

Print at 100% ("Actual size") and check the line at the top measures 10 cm. Lay each tag flat on top of its robot, facing up, with the printed arrow pointing toward the robot's face. python tag_view.py --source 0 shows which tags the camera can read.

7. Check the robot sizes

The tracker knows roughly how big each robot is. It uses this to report the robot's middle, to draw a box around each robot in the preview, to keep the robots inside the arena, and to ignore the robots when it looks for the balloon.

The sizes are estimates. Measure your robots with a ruler and change them in tracker/room.json (made by step 5), under "robots", in metres:

SettingCozmoMiPWhat it is
length0.100.09Front to back (Cozmo: include the lift arms)
width0.060.11Side to side (MiP: wheel to wheel)
height0.0750.20Floor to the top
tag_height0.070.20Floor to the marker on top
tag_forward-0.020.0How far the marker is in front of the robot's middle (negative means behind)

If the box in the preview is bigger or smaller than the robot, or off to one side, adjust these numbers.

8. Teach it the balloon

The tracker finds the balloon by its colour, and it starts set for yellow. Move anything else yellow out of the camera's view first. A yellow tennis ball by the arena fooled ours.

  1. Run the tracker with colour sliders: 
    python tracker.py --source 0 --tune
  2. Hold the balloon in view. Drag the sliders until only the balloon shows white, then press S to save.
  3. Rest the balloon on the floor in view and press F. This measures its width, which the tracker uses to work out how high the balloon is.

Check: ours measured 32.7 cm (about 13 in) wide. Resting on the floor, the tracker should then read its height as about half its width (16 cm for ours), because the height is measured to the balloon's centre.

Other keys: T shows or hides the sliders, and Q quits. Pictures are processed in memory and never saved.

9. Test the robots on their own

Do these before the games, from the main keepy-up-code folder (cd .. if you're in tracker). None of these use the camera, so the robots can't see: give each robot about 2 × 2 m of clear floor and keep children out of the way.

Connect the robots

  • MiP uses Bluetooth. Stand it up and let it balance first. MiP has no edge sensor, so only ever run it on the floor.
  • Cozmo makes its own Wi-Fi network. Put him on his charger to wake him, then join his network from the laptop's Wi-Fi menu: his face shows the network name and password. Both robots can be connected at once.

If Cozmo connects but won't move (no face, no wheels), his software may have fallen back to a recovery mode. Connect him once with the official Cozmo app on a phone or tablet, which updates his software, then try again. That fixed ours.

Sounds

The download has no sound files, because they come from other companies' apps. Everything still works without them, just silently.

  • Cozmo's voice: PyCozmo plays Cozmo's animations without sound, so the games play Cozmo's own voice clips alongside. If you have the Cozmo app on a Mac, sounds/cozmo/README.txt explains where they are and how to convert them.
  • Game sound effects (soccer): add your own short, quiet .wav files to the sounds folder. sounds/README.txt lists the names the game looks for.
  • MiP's sounds are built into MiP, so nothing to add.

The tests, in order

python3 mip_test.py                   # MiP: light, small turns, short roll, battery
python3 voice_start.py --no-robots    # voice only: "Let's play", "Stop", "All done"
python3 voice_start.py --no-cozmo     # voice + MiP
python3 voice_start.py                # voice + both robots
python3 robot_tests.py clap           # clap once to roll, say "stop" to freeze
python3 robot_tests.py start          # say "start" or "let's play" to roll
python3 robot_tests.py stop           # say "start", wait, then "stop"

In the voice and robot tests, the Space bar pauses, like the big pause button, and Q quits. Add --no-cozmo, --no-mip or --no-robots to leave a robot out.

10. Play keepy uppy

How it plays

  • The children keep the balloon up in the air. The robots don't hit it: they follow it on the floor.
  • The pink centre line splits the arena. Cozmo looks after the left half, MiP the right.
  • The robot whose half the balloon is over drives to the spot on the floor under it. The other robot waits where it is.
  • The blue squares in the back corners are the robots' rest squares. The robots don't drive to them by themselves yet.

Run it

You need two Terminal windows, both in the tracker folder with the environment switched on (step 2).

Terminal 1 runs the tracker:

python tracker.py --source 0

Terminal 2 runs the robots. Start with a dry run, where nothing moves and it prints where the robots would go:

python chase.py --dry-run

Then with one robot at a time, then both:

python chase.py --only cozmo
python chase.py --only mip
python chase.py

The top speed is 0.10 m/s; change it with --max-speed. Press Ctrl+C in Terminal 2 to stop the robots.

11. Play floor soccer

Two people play Cozmo and MiP, air-hockey style, on the same arena. A friendly game screen starts everything: the camera, the scoreboard and the robots. The rules, how the robots play and how to start it are on the Floor soccer page.

12. Safety and known limits

Every robot stops at once if:

  • the camera hasn't seen it, or the balloon, for half a second
  • it's hidden from the camera under the balloon (it waits there)
  • no message has come from the tracker for half a second
  • you press Ctrl+C

The robots always stay inside the arena: a robot found outside it drives straight back in before doing anything else.

Known limits right now:

  • People and children aren't tracked, so the robots don't slow down or stop near them. A grown-up always watches, and speeds stay low.
  • Keepy uppy hasn't been played with the real robots yet, and it doesn't have the game screen, voice commands or sounds that floor soccer has.
  • In keepy uppy, the robots don't drive to their blue rest squares by themselves yet.
  • In soccer, MiP gets weak when its battery is low: the game screen warns below 25%, so charge it before playing.

13. For programmers: the position stream

tracker.py streams positions on ws://localhost:8765, about 15 times a second, so you can build your own game on top:

{"type": "positions", "t": 1730412345.12,
 "room": {"width": 3.05, "depth": 1.63, "clockwise": true, "balloon_size": 0.327},
 "robots": {"cozmo": {"id": 1, "x": 0.8, "y": 1.2, "heading": 90.0,
                      "length": 0.10, "width": 0.06, "height": 0.075, "seen": true, "age": 0.03},
            "mip":   {"id": 2, "x": 2.1, "y": 0.9, "heading": 270.0,
                      "length": 0.09, "width": 0.11, "height": 0.20, "seen": true, "age": 0.03}},
 "balloon": {"x": 1.4, "y": 0.6, "height": 1.1, "seen": true, "age": 0.03}}
  • Positions are in metres from cross 1 (back-left). x runs toward cross 2, and y toward cross 4 (the front).
  • A robot's x and y are its middle. length, width and height are its size.
  • heading 0 means facing cross 2, and 90 means facing the front. With this cross order, heading goes up clockwise seen from above, so "clockwise": true: anything that steers a robot must flip its turn direction.
  • seen turns false after half a second without a sighting. A robot hidden under the balloon keeps its last spot for up to 2 seconds, with "hidden": true.
  • The balloon's height is to its centre, worked out from its width (balloon_size).

More detail is in tracker/README.md and at the top of each script.
