Floor soccer

The second game on the Keepy Up arena: two people against Cozmo and MiP, played like air hockey. The two people kneel behind their own goal line and tap the balloon with their hands, while the robots push it with their bodies. Each team defends its own goal, and the ceiling camera spots goals and keeps the score.

Before playing, read the safety rules. The robots don't see people, so a grown-up always watches.

Safety

The rules

  1. Two teams: two people against Cozmo and MiP. The people kneel behind their own goal line, outside the arena, and can lean over the line as far as they like. They never step in. The robots may cross the centre line, but only up to their reach line, halfway into the people's half.
  2. Nobody kicks. People hit or tap the balloon with their hands and arms, and the robots push it with their bodies. People's arms hit harder than the robots push.
  3. Serving: the scoreboard shows whose turn it is with "People's balloon" or "Robot's balloon". A new game starts with the people's balloon, served toward the robots. For the robot's balloon, a person puts it down on the robots' side, and the robots go back to their pink squares, facing the people's goal. Once the balloon has sat on the floor on that side for about a second, the scoreboard says "Play!".
  4. A goal counts as soon as the balloon, rolling on the floor from inside the arena, touches the pink goal line between the two pink posts. A balloon in the air, one carried back in through the goal, or one put down on the line at a serve doesn't count.
  5. After a goal, the team that scored gets the balloon.
  6. "Out!" is called once the balloon has been outside the arena for half a second. The team whose side it went out on gets the balloon.
  7. "Stuck!" If the balloon doesn't move for 10 seconds, somewhere nobody can reach, the scoreboard says "Stuck!" and the team whose side it's on serves again.
  8. First team to 5 goals wins.

How a game starts

Every game, first or new, begins the same calm way, on both the game screen and the scoreboard:

  1. "Get ready..."
  2. 1 (beep)
  3. 2 (beep)
  4. 3 (beep)
  5. START!

The robots wait, and don't move until START!.

How the robots play

  • Two long strips: the arena is split in two the long way. Cozmo has the back strip and guards the back half of the goal; MiP has the front strip and guards the front half.
  • Going for the balloon: when the balloon is in a robot's strip and within reach, that robot gets behind it and pushes it toward the people's goal. Either robot can score.
  • Waiting: during play, a robot that isn't going for the balloon waits where it is. If it's over the centre line, it first goes back just over it. The robots go back to their pink squares only at a serve, and face the people's goal there.
  • Knocking it free: if a robot can't get behind a balloon resting by the tape, it drives through it to knock it back into play.
  • Same speed: both robots have the same top speed, 0.20 m/s, which is Cozmo's fastest.
  • Keeping apart: neither robot enters the other's strip. When they're close (35 cm), the robot that isn't going for the balloon won't drive toward the other, and when they're very close (20 cm) neither does. In simulation they never got closer than 19 cm.
  • Staying inside the tape: the robots keep about 20 cm inside the purple border and slow down near it.

Cozmo's and MiP's personalities

  • Cozmo's eyes blink as usual while he plays.
  • Cozmo's faces change at game moments: happy at "Play!" and a new game, surprised at "Out!", and sad when the people score.
  • Cozmo's arms give a small lift at happy moments and when he sets off after the balloon.
  • Cozmo's sounds: a quiet hum or giggle every so often while he plays, and a "vroom" when he sets off after the balloon.
  • Both robots react quietly in their own voices to goals, "Out!", wins and a new game.

The game screen

A button on the Mac's desktop, Keepy Up Soccer, opens one calm screen that runs the whole game:

  1. Choose the robots: A both, C Cozmo, M MiP. It remembers your last choice.
  2. A checklist of what to do before playing. It checks the Mac is on Cozmo's Wi-Fi, and says when to move Cozmo from his charger into his pink square.
  3. During the game: the score in big numbers, what each robot is doing, and MiP's battery, with a red warning under 25%.
  4. At the end: a summary, and the choice to play again.
KeyVoiceWhat it does
Return"start"Start the game. Saying "start" also carries on after a pause.
PPause, or carry on
NNew game: back to 0–0, with the start sequence
Q"stop"Stop the game. The robots stop their wheels first.

Voice commands are offline: the laptop's microphone listens for "start" and "stop" only, and nothing is recorded or saved. Only one game window can be open at a time, and closing it stops the robots.

Making it reliable

The Mac uses one radio for both Cozmo's Wi-Fi and MiP's Bluetooth. Before these fixes, MiP dropped out several times a minute when both robots played:

  • MiP reconnects by itself when its Bluetooth drops, and the game carries on. Cozmo keeps playing meanwhile. In the first full game it dropped 4 times and came back each time.
  • Less Wi-Fi traffic to Cozmo: the game only sends Cozmo a new wheel command when it changes.
  • Cozmo's software had fallen back to a recovery mode, where he connected but wouldn't move. Connecting him once with the official Cozmo app updated it.
  • Cozmo starts on his charger, and the game screen says when to move him into the arena.

Goal width

We're keeping the goals at 72 cm, as taped. In simulation with our real balloon and both robots guarding, 72 cm goals were very hard to score on:

1 m35

Real people play differently from the simulation, and people did score in the robot tests. We'll check in full games with people, and widen the goals if it's too hard: move the pink posts, then change GOAL_WIDTH in tracker/room.py to the new width in metres.

Run it

First set up the camera, arena, robot markers and balloon: steps 1–9 of Run it yourself.

The easy way: the game screen

In Terminal, from the tracker folder with the environment switched on (step 2 of Run it yourself):

python play_soccer.py

It starts the camera, the scoreboard and the robots for you. To try it with no robots moving, add --dry-run. To turn the microphone off, add --no-voice.

Make a desktop button, like ours

On our Mac, a Keepy Up Soccer button on the desktop opens the game screen with a double-click. To make your own, save these lines in a plain text file on your desktop called Keepy Up Soccer.command, changing the first cd line if your code is somewhere else:

#!/bin/bash
cd ~/keepy-up/tracker || exit 1
exec .venv/bin/python play_soccer.py "$@"

Then, in Terminal, make it runnable once:

chmod +x ~/Desktop/"Keepy Up Soccer.command"

The first time you double-click it, macOS may ask: right-click it, choose Open, then Open again.

The game keeps a log of each game on the laptop, in the logs folder. Nothing is sent anywhere, and no pictures or sound are saved.

By hand, in three Terminal windows

Terminal 1, the tracker:

python tracker.py --source 0

Terminal 2, the referee and scoreboard. The robots defend the right-hand goal:

python soccer.py

Terminal 3, the robots. Start with a dry run, then the real robots:

python chase.py --game soccer --max-speed 0.20 --max-turn 180 --dry-run
python chase.py --game soccer --max-speed 0.20 --max-turn 180

Scoreboard keys: Space pauses, R starts a new game, K serves again, and Q quits. Press Ctrl+C in Terminal 3 to stop the robots.

Before playing, go through the safety rules above.
