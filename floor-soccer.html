Floor soccer

The second game on the Keepy Up arena: two people against Cozmo and MiP, played like air hockey. Each team defends its own goal, nobody crosses the centre line, and only the balloon goes from side to side. The camera spots goals and keeps the score.

The rules

  1. Two teams: two people against Cozmo and MiP. Each team stays on its own side of the pink centre line. Only the balloon crosses.
  2. Nobody kicks. People push the balloon along the floor with their hands, and the robots push it with their bodies. That's why the start of play is called a serve, not a kick-off.
  3. A new game starts with the people serving toward the robots.
  4. A goal counts when the balloon, on the floor, crosses the goal line between the posts. A balloon in the air doesn't count.
  5. After a goal, the team that scored serves.
  6. After "Out!" (the balloon leaves the arena anywhere else), the team whose side it went out on serves.
  7. First team to 5 goals wins.

When it's the robots' serve, a person puts the balloon down on the robots' side. Once the balloon has sat on the floor on the serving side for about a second, the scoreboard says "Play!".

How the robots defend

The robots' half is split into two lanes, so the two robots never bump into each other:

  • Cozmo has the back lane and guards the back half of the goal.
  • MiP has the front lane and guards the front half of the goal.
  • Each robot slides along just in front of the goal line, staying between the balloon and its half of the goal.
  • When the balloon comes into a robot's lane, that robot goes out, gets behind it and pushes it back toward the people's goal.
  • Neither robot ever leaves its lane, crosses the centre line or leaves the arena.

In simulation, guarding robots let in about a third fewer goals than robots standing still, and they always stayed at least 28 cm apart.

Goal width

We're keeping the goals at 72 cm, as taped. In simulation with our real balloon and both robots guarding, 72 cm goals were very hard to score on:

Real people play differently from the simulation, so we'll check in real games. If people can't score, we'll widen the goals: move the pink posts, then change GOAL_WIDTH in tracker/room.py to the new width in metres, so the scoreboard and robots match the tape.

Run it

First set up the camera, arena, robot markers and balloon: steps 1–9 of Run it yourself. Then open three Terminal windows, all in the tracker folder with the environment switched on.

Terminal 1 runs the tracker:

python tracker.py --source 0

Terminal 2 runs the referee and scoreboard. The robots defend the right-hand goal and the people the left:

python soccer.py

Scoreboard keys: Space pauses or resumes, R starts a new game, K serves again, and Q quits. To swap ends, add --robots-defend left here and in Terminal 3.

Terminal 3 runs the robots. Start with a dry run, where nothing moves and it prints where the robots would go:

python chase.py --game soccer --dry-run

Then the real robots, one at a time, then both:

python chase.py --game soccer --only cozmo
python chase.py --game soccer

Press Ctrl+C in Terminal 3 to stop the robots. The same safety stops as keepy uppy apply: see Safety and known limits.
